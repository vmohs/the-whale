Making of the Whale

venus mohs is based in providence, ri. venus is graduating with a bfa in film/animation/video (open media). venus practices making permanent marks– fossilizing moments, ruining videos forever, documenting emotions, scarring themselves and the world around them. venus works in visual poetry, painting, printmaking, drawing and videos with text elements. small fragments combine into shrines and altars, degraded videos with posterized colors, monolith bodies of text. venus is interested in illegibility and abstraction, the eroticism of pushing an image so far that it becomes irrecognizable and stripped down to the point where only feeling and catharsis remain. venus works mechanically, repetitively and ritualistically to stitch together an atmosphere in their work where art and beauty become religion and worship, the sacred becoming evident through the profane. the content of their work is encrypted such that specificity becomes a weapon, personal details become universal through off putting and uncanny imagery. venus wants to put thousands of hours into something that only they can understand intimately and everyone else is left guessing as a form of release. to burden the viewer with secrets that cannot be deciphered. the violent decay of video becomes a tool for them to obscure and highlight, secrets remain hidden and only they know where. footage is blurry and overexposed but the feeling is there and explicit, like a pit in your stomach. venus' work is evidence and footprints– gestural traces of uncomfortable emotions and gruesome bruises on a screen. obsessive, vulnerable reflections of how they see the world– in this light, in these colors, immortalized. venus is burning something down and finding overwhelming meaning in the ash.
